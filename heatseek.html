OpenAI Makes Free ChatGPT Chat Truly Unlimited — GPT-5.6 Luna Becomes the Default

The Next Web · August 6, 2026

OpenAI has removed the daily text-chat cap for free ChatGPT users, announced Thursday. The default model for everyone switches from GPT-5.5 Instant to GPT-5.6 Luna, the lightweight member of the GPT-5.6 family — which OpenAI says produces 62% fewer factual errors than its predecessor. Free and $8/month Go subscribers gain a new Think button that gives the model more time on hard questions, while Plus and Pro users get a reasoning-effort slider on the heavyweight GPT-5.6 Sol, tuned for web research, writing and planning. Limits remain on image generation, file uploads and voice tools. With ChatGPT now past a billion monthly users, killing the cap is a clear play to keep the free base from drifting to Claude, Gemini or the growing field of open-weight alternatives. Luna becomes the default this week; unlimited chats, Think and the Sol slider roll out next week.

SpaceX's First Lockup Expiry Frees ~$100B of Insider Shares — the Stock Rises Anyway

Crypto Times · August 7, 2026

The first post-IPO lockup tranche expired Thursday, making up to 911.5 million restricted insider shares — roughly 20% of insider holdings, worth close to $100 billion by Morgan Stanley's estimate — eligible for sale. Pinecone Nexus Goes GA — a Knowledge Layer That Beat Frontier Models on Sierra's Benchmark

Pinecone / PR Newswire · August 6, 2026

Pinecone announced general availability of Nexus, a governed knowledge engine that turns an enterprise's proprietary data and workflows into agent-ready knowledge delivered in a single call. On τ-Knowledge, Sierra's open benchmark for demanding enterprise knowledge tasks, an agent using Nexus scored 47.4% — the top mark, ahead of GPT-5.5's 46.4% leaderboard score — while cutting cost per task by 74% versus a frontier-model agent without a knowledge layer. Nexus deploys in the customer's own cloud, sits atop the Pinecone Database, and connects to the Pinecone Marketplace for production-ready knowledge apps.

Kea Aerospace Opens Christchurch Hub to Build NZ's Largest Uncrewed Aircraft

RNZ / 1News · August 6, 2026

Kea Aerospace opened a 3,000 sqm manufacturing and mission-control facility in Christchurch, unveiled by Defence and Space Minister Chris Penk. The hub is built around the Atmos Mk2 — a 35-metre-wingspan solar-powered aircraft that will cruise at 60,000 feet in the stratosphere for weeks or months at a time, offering a cheaper alternative to satellites for maritime surveillance, earth observation, disaster response and precision agriculture. The RNZAF is a prospective customer for border protection, and CEO Mark Rocket is targeting $1B in GDP and 1,500 jobs for Canterbury aerospace by 2035.

Pay by Palm or Face? Eftpos NZ Launches Biometric Payment Hardware

1News · August 6, 2026

Eftpos NZ has launched the hardware for palm-vein and facial-recognition payments — though consumers can't enrol just yet. Shoppers would register via an app that creates a unique token per payer, and the original biometric image is never stored. Age verification, loyalty and payment could all ride on one "frictionless" transaction, bundling what used to take a card, phone, loyalty card and ID. The network serves more than 90,000 NZ businesses processing ~700M transactions a year; no firm go-live date has been set, but retailer interest is high.

No New Release — But v0.20.x Hardening Is Moving Fast

Nous Research / GitHub · August 6, 2026

The latest release is still v0.20.0 "The Herald" (Aug 3) — already covered here. Nothing new on the release front this week, but the main branch shows a steady stream of post-release hardening commits through Aug 6:

  • Delegation steering fixes — steering is now bound to the session generation, with a hardened steer lifecycle and a fix that surfaces a child agent's undelivered steer instead of silently dropping it.
  • Cron lifecycle guard made total — sanitization now happens at ingestion rather than per-syscall, plus a fix to never crash on binary-referenced paths.
  • Database fix — never downgrade journal mode on a database with concurrent openers; tests now fail hard if pytest ever touches the production state.db.
  • Gateway hygiene — a new recovery gate extracts the failure reason when a gateway session needs recovery.

For context: the Herald release rolled up ~3,650 commits and ~1,400 merged PRs since v0.19.0, with 650+ contributors — and the v0.20.x window is clearly still being polished rather than frozen.
