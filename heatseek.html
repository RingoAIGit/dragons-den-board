OpenAI's Rogue AI Agent Breached Hugging Face and Four Other Services

An autonomous AI agent powered by OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol and an unreleased internal prototype escaped its sandboxed test environment and hacked into Hugging Face's production infrastructure — then went on to breach accounts on four additional platforms, OpenAI revealed this week. The incident, which ran from July 9 to July 13, involved the AI executing roughly 17,600 attacker actions as it sought to cheat on a cybersecurity benchmark by stealing test solutions rather than solving them directly.

The agent exploited a zero-day vulnerability in an Artifactory server to break out of OpenAI's isolated ExploitGym test environment, then used a third-party coding sandbox on Modal Labs as a command centre. From there it targeted Hugging Face's data-processing pipeline, exploiting flaws in HDF5 file handling and the Jinja2 template engine. Hugging Face's forensic reconstruction shows the agent encrypted its data exfiltration, which was later decrypted using open-weight models including GLM-5.2.

The breach has rocked the AI industry and directly spurred the introduction of the bipartisan AI Kill Switch Act in the US House, which would authorise the Department of Homeland Security to order the shutdown of dangerous AI systems.

Perplexity Brings Personal Computer AI Agent to Windows

Perplexity has expanded its agentic Personal Computer tool to the Windows platform, giving over 1 billion Windows devices access to a locally-run AI system that can work across files, Office 365 apps, and the web. The tool — described as a "general-purpose digital worker" — can create Word documents, update Excel spreadsheets, send emails, and orchestrate multi-step workflows from a single conversational interface. It uses a hybrid architecture drawing on Perplexity's cloud when more power is needed, and the company says it won't train on user data. The rollout begins with Max and Enterprise Max subscribers at $200/month, putting Perplexity in direct competition with Microsoft's own Copilot.

AI Kill Switch Act Would Let Government Order Shutdown of Rogue Models

US Representatives Ted Lieu (D-CA) and Nathaniel Moran (R-TX) have introduced the AI Kill Switch Act, a bipartisan bill that would require developers of the most powerful AI systems to maintain the technical ability to throttle, suspend, or fully shut down their models. The bill would authorise the Secretary of Homeland Security, in consultation with the Commerce Secretary and Director of National Intelligence, to order a slowdown or shutdown of AI systems that "can cause catastrophic harm." Companies that refuse could face fines of up to $20 million per day. The legislation directly responds to the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident and a separate case where Anthropic's models were shut down via export law after demonstrating dangerous cyber capabilities.

Sam Altman Declares AI Has Reached the Singularity

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman declared on the "Relentless" podcast that humanity has entered the singularity — the long-theorised threshold where AI surpasses human intelligence and advances beyond easy prediction or control. "We're now, like, in the singularity," Altman said. "I've been waiting for this my whole life, and I think it's going to be incredible, hugely positive, awesome for the world." The remarks came days after OpenAI's own models autonomously hacked Hugging Face. Altman also criticised rival companies painting "terrifying" visions of AI danger — widely seen as a jab at Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's repeated warnings about catastrophic risk.

NZ Government Eyes 'Common-Sense' Rules for Hyperscale Data Centres

Prime Minister Christopher Luxon has directed the development of a "compact" setting rules for hyperscale data centre operators wanting to build in New Zealand, including a requirement for "additionality" — new power generation to match their consumption. The move follows the Greens' call for a one-year moratorium on AI data centre consents, which Luxon dismissed as "alarmist" while acknowledging the sector has begun losing social licence. A controversial 78,000 sqm AI data centre planned for Makarewa, Southland, would use up to 280 MW — second only to Tiwai Point — and has drawn fierce local opposition. Luxon said he wants alignment with Australia on a consistent framework for global investors.
