AI Health

Microsoft's AI Panel Beats Doctors Twice Over on the Hardest Cases

A study in Science finds MAI-DxO, a panel of AI agents that question the patient, order tests and debate the answer, diagnosed 83% of 304 tricky New England Journal of Medicine cases. Generalist doctors with textbooks and the internet got 36%; without them, 22%. On 325 ordinary emergency cases the gap narrows to 80% against 47%. Best results ran on OpenAI's GPT-5, and Microsoft's own data shows most of the gain comes from the stronger model rather than the orchestration: DxO added about four accuracy points and cut test costs. The caveats matter. Doctors were generalists, cases were text only. A real hospital trial at Beth Israel Deaconess starts within months.

AI Safety

Outside Testers Broke OpenAI's Agent Watchdog

METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks embedded inside OpenAI, from 21 July to 11 August. They found an exploit that let agents hide hundreds of recent actions from the misalignment monitor, a route around Codex sandboxing and Auto-Review, and two settings that switch monitoring off for new agents. They estimate only 75–94% of internal traffic from GPT-5.5-class models was even eligible for monitoring. OpenAI says the production holes are patched. METR has not verified that.

AI Research

Claude Wrote 180 Million Lines of Proof That Code Won't Crash

Working with Theorem, Claude produced machine-checked Rocq proofs that 239 fuzz entry points across 123 open-source libraries never crash on any input under 64MB. The work sits at RISC-V assembly level and took a few weeks. Along the way it found and patched hundreds of bugs that fuzzing had missed for years. The limits are real: single-threaded static RISC-V binaries with musl, not the x86 or ARM builds people actually run. Anthropic's own framing holds — formally verified is not bug-free.

Uber Puts All 30,000 Staff on ChatGPT Work

Uber has rolled its agentic ChatGPT Work out company-wide, up from a 1,000-person pilot. It plugs into Gmail, Drive, Calendar and Slack and defaults to GPT-6.1 Sol. Four in five of the pilot users were non-engineers, using it for data analysis, localising training material and building their own dashboards. The timing is uncomfortable: it lands weeks after Uber cut about 3,300 corporate roles. Uber won't say what the rollout costs and will track usage instead.
