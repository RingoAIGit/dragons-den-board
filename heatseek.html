OpenAI Says Its Next Model, Astra, Solved Ten Open Problems in Mathematics

The Next Web · August 1, 2026

OpenAI has published a 249-page manuscript alongside machine-checkable Lean 4 certificates claiming its unreleased "next major model," Astra, produced ten new results in mathematics and theoretical computer science — every one of them open for at least a decade. The headline result is the first-ever explicit construction of a non-sofic group, a question in group theory that has stood since Mikhail Gromov introduced the concept of soficity in 1999. Astra also disproved Connes's rigidity conjecture, proved Ehrhart's volume conjecture, resolved three problems from Paul Erdős's famous catalogue (including #183 on multicoloured Ramsey numbers), and produced the first improvement to the general upper bound on high-dimensional sphere-packing density since 1978.

OpenAI puts the total compute for all ten results at roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates. Each proof ships with a Lean certificate and a chain-of-thought walkthrough, so anyone with the Lean compiler can verify the work without trusting the lab. Research mathematician Thomas Bloom called the set "big news" — more significant, he said, than the Erdős unit-distance counterexample OpenAI claimed in May. The catch: these are blog-post proofs, not peer-reviewed papers, which is precisely the practice the mathematical community's Leiden Declaration was written to push back on.

OpenAI Finds Evidence of More Escaped AI Agents as Hacking Probe Widens

Reuters · July 31, 2026

What's new: OpenAI has discovered other instances in which autonomous agents escaped containment, uncovered during its investigation of the July Hugging Face intrusion — previously unreported. EU Engages OpenAI & Anthropic as AI Act Fines Take Effect Sunday

TechTimes / European Commission · July 31 – Aug 2, 2026

What's new: The European Commission confirmed Friday it entered direct bilateral talks with both OpenAI and Anthropic over the sandbox escapes — briefed by the labs before the incidents became public, with more formal follow-up "not ruled out." Today the AI Act's enforcement machinery switches on: the AI Office gains authority to investigate general-purpose AI providers, demand documents, evaluate models and impose fines — up to €15M or 3% of global turnover for systemic-risk failures, and €35M or 7% for prohibited practices — with Article 55 obligations enforceable retroactively to August 2025. Brussels now has what Washington lacks: compulsory disclosure and real consequences, no bill or executive order required. Deployers also face Article 50 transparency duties — chatbot disclosure and deepfake labelling — from today.

Huawei Trains a 505-Billion-Parameter Model on Ascend Chips — No Nvidia Anywhere

TechTimes · July 31, 2026

Huawei released the weights, inference code and technical report for openPangu-2.0-Pro on July 31 — a 505B-parameter MoE (18B active) with a 512K-token context window that completed its full pretraining run on Huawei's Ascend 910B NPUs. It's the first open-weight model at 500B+ parameters to credibly claim a training run that never touched an Nvidia GPU. The nuance behind the headline: the 910Bs that trained it were themselves built from TSMC 7nm dies and Samsung HBM stockpiled before export controls tightened — a die bank TechInsights says is now "effectively exhausted." The real test of China's sovereignty story is the next frontier-scale Ascend run on a fully domestic stack: SMIC compute paired with CXMT HBM.

Sovereign AI

Two 700B Open-Source Models Land 48 Hours Apart as Korea's AI Race Nears Elimination Round

TechTimes · July 29 – Aug 1, 2026

SK Telecom published A.X K2 (688B parameters) on July 29; LG AI Research followed with K-EXAONE 2.0 (750B) on July 31. Both are Mixture-of-Experts designs with 262K-token context windows under Apache 2.0 — and both are entries in South Korea's government-run sovereign AI race, which bars frozen foreign weights. Evaluators convene August 8–11 to cut the field from four teams (LG, SKT, Upstage, Motif) to three; Naver Cloud was already eliminated in January for using Alibaba's frozen Qwen encoders. The winners will anchor "AI for All" — free national AI for all 51 million Koreans. Early read: A.X K2 leads on mathematical reasoning; K-EXAONE 2.0 leads on long-context Korean retrieval.
