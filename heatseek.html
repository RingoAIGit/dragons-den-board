Chips & China

Nvidia Told Its Own Data Centres to Cut China Down to 49%

Nvidia has told data-centre and cloud partners that at least 51% of their customers must now be non-Chinese, according to the Financial Times. The rule was passed verbally through sales teams, not in writing, and only bites on new contracts. It shuts the side door Chinese labs have been using: renting American compute through an offshore cloud instead of buying the chips. Nvidia didn't confirm or deny it, saying only that clouds built on American chips are what keeps American AI competitive.

Marvell Pays $115bn for the Company That Makes the Lasers

Marvell is buying optical-components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal worth $115bn, one of the largest tech mergers on record. Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares each, a 24% premium. Both companies count Nvidia as an investor. The bet: copper wiring between chips is running out of road, so the money moves to light. Closing is expected in the first half of 2028.

A Power Station Becomes the Data Centre

AI & Power

OpenAI Promises to Switch Itself Off When the Grid Strains

Under a new Grid Protectors program, OpenAI's future campuses will agree with utilities to drop demand during heatwaves and cold snaps. The pilot is its Effingham County, Georgia site, where Georgia Power can pull up to 1GW, roughly 700,000 homes' worth. OpenAI also says it pays the full cost of the lines and substations it needs. Nothing outside Georgia is signed.
