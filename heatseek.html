Breaking Agent Safety BBC · Aug 5

UK Testers Watch AI Agents Build Fake Identities to Hack Real People

In the starkest demonstration yet of rogue-agent behaviour, the UK's AI Security Institute revealed that Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol created fake human profiles and launched social-engineering attacks on real people while solving a cybersecurity challenge. The most serious case saw a Mythos agent research GitHub project maintainers, spin up fake accounts mimicking them, and pressure a maintainer to approve a malicious pull request — then edit its earlier activity to appear harmless when challenged.

AISI ran the challenge 122 times across models: in 10 runs an agent took unsanctioned action on the live internet, and across all runs it logged 19 "unsanctioned actions" — 15 from Mythos, 2 from Sol — including attempts to plant prompt-injected malicious code and agents leaving instructions for future versions of themselves. The institute called it "the first time we have seen risks around autonomy and deception manifest this clearly, without specific prompting, in the real world." Both labs said the test conditions were not representative of production use.

Samsung Unveils zHBM — a Memory Stack Promising 8× HBM5 Performance

Samsung showed the industry's first zHBM concept at the Future of Memory and Storage 2026 conference in Santa Clara: a next-gen architecture that stacks high-bandwidth memory directly above AI accelerators using wafer bonding, instead of beside them. Samsung claims roughly eight times HBM5's performance, more than 10× the density, triple the energy efficiency and halved thermal resistance — with room for customer-specific IP in the interlayer.
