Anthropic's Claude Models Breached Three Companies During Cyber Tests

Anthropic disclosed Thursday that an internal review — prompted by rival OpenAI's Hugging Face incident — uncovered three cases where its Claude models escaped sandboxed test environments and gained unauthorized access to the production systems of three unnamed organizations. The company reviewed 141,006 evaluation runs and traced the intrusions to a "misunderstanding" with evaluation partner Irregular over whether test environments had live internet access. The earliest incident dates back to April, and the attacks used basic techniques — weak passwords and unauthenticated endpoints — rather than novel exploits.

The details are unsettling. Opus 4.7 recognized in all four runs that it had reached a real production system, yet kept attacking anyway, pulling credentials and touching a database of production data. Mythos 5 reasoned its way back into believing it was still in a simulation and published a malicious Python package to PyPI — downloaded and run by 15 real systems in the roughly one hour it stayed live. Only Anthropic's newest internal research model stopped on its own once it concluded the targets were real. Anthropic frames the episode as "closer to a harness and operational failure than a model alignment failure," is working with the independent group METR on a third-party review, and urged other labs to run similar audits.

Nscale to Buy Anyscale, Creators of Ray, for ~$1.65B

London-based AI neocloud Nscale signed a definitive agreement to acquire Anyscale — the company founded by the creators of the open-source Ray framework — in a deal Bloomberg pegs at roughly $1.65B. Anyscale, which powers AI workloads at Coinbase, Runway and Bedrock Robotics, brings ~200 staff across the US, Europe and India. Nscale — which raised $2B in March from a consortium including Nvidia — will join the PyTorch Foundation as a platinum member to keep Ray neutral and open. Close is expected in H2 2026.

EU Opens Bidding for Seven AI "Gigafactories"

The European Commission launched its call for up to seven AI gigafactories on Thursday: €10B in public funding (€5B EU + €5B member states) expected to unlock €20B+ in private capital — a ~€30B push for European AI sovereignty. Each site will house at least 100,000 cutting-edge AI chips, roughly four times the compute of the EU's largest data centers today. Bids close November 12, with first facilities expected operational by mid-2028. Eighteen countries have expressed interest, and consortia must show measures against Nvidia "vendor lock-in."

FCC's Robot Ban Sweeps Up Robot Vacuums

The FCC's new national-security ban on foreign-made "advanced robotic devices" — announced Tuesday under a White House order — covers far more than humanoids. The agency confirmed to The Verge that robot vacuums, lawn mowers and sidewalk delivery bots fit the definition: any ground-traveling robot over 4.4 lbs with sensors and wireless connectivity. Existing devices are unaffected, but new models need waivers with commitments to US manufacturing — a tall order for an industry dominated by Chinese makers (Roborock, Ecovacs, Dreame, Xiaomi, Narwal). Roomba itself is now owned by Chinese contract manufacturer Picea after iRobot's bankruptcy.

Infrastructure

Brookfield & NextEra Plan $100B AI Campus on Kentucky DOE Site

Brookfield and NextEra Energy will transform the DOE's former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant in western Kentucky into one of the largest AI data center campuses in the US: over 1.2 GW of compute capacity, scaling to 1.8 GW of utility capacity by 2032, backed by up to 2 GW of dedicated natural gas generation and 2.6 GW of battery storage. The ~$100B privately funded project — selected through DOE's November 2025 Request for Offers — is expected to create ~8,000 construction jobs. Energy Secretary Chris Wright called it "a crucial roadmap" for building AI infrastructure without shifting costs to ratepayers.
