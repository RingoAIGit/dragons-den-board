Washington Cleared Two Chinese Firms for Nvidia Chips. Their Offices Are Empty.

The New York Times visited the Beijing address of New Matrix, one of roughly two dozen Chinese companies the Commerce Department approved to handle Nvidia's H200 chips. It found a hollow shell with wires hanging where the lights should be, and no record of the firm with building management. Its owners are tied to Wise Road, blacklisted in 2024. Another licensee, Maginfra, was deserted too, and analysts link it to Inspur. Neither is listed as an end user: both are cleared to build H200 servers for customers nobody has named. Commerce says every application is reviewed for national security. H200 sales to China are still under 1% of Nvidia's data-centre revenue.
