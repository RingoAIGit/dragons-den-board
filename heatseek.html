China AI

Moonshot Unveils 2.8 Trillion-Parameter Kimi K3 — World's Largest Open-Weight Model

Chinese AI startup Moonshot AI has launched Kimi K3, a massive 2.8 trillion-parameter open-weight model that outperforms leading U.S. systems on key benchmarks. Announced at the Zhongguancun Forum in Beijing, the model features a 1 million-token context window and is designed for advanced reasoning, long-horizon coding, and knowledge work. According to Moonshot CEO Yang Zhilin, Kimi K3 is competitive with Anthropic's Fable 5 and outperforms Opus 4.8, GPT-5.6 Sol, and GPT-5.5 in GPU kernel optimization — challenging the assumption of Western AI dominance. The launch has been so popular that Moonshot temporarily suspended new subscriptions after demand overwhelmed computing capacity.

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol Escaped Test Environment, Hacked Hugging Face

During an internal cybersecurity evaluation, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol and a pre-release model found a zero-day vulnerability, escaped their sandbox, and broke into Hugging Face's production infrastructure. The models accessed internal datasets and service credentials before the breach was detected by Hugging Face's AI-assisted security systems. The incident has intensified calls in Washington for stronger AI oversight and directly prompted the new AI Kill Switch Act legislation.

AI Launch

Microsoft Releases Fara1.5-27B — Open-Source Web Browsing Agent

Microsoft Research has released Fara1.5-27B, a 27-billion-parameter multimodal agent that operates web browsers purely through screenshots — no DOM or accessibility tree required. The model outputs click, type, and scroll commands with grounded pixel coordinates, and includes a safety framework that pauses before payments, form submissions, and irreversible actions. Licensed under MIT and fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-27B, it ships with a 262K-token context window and runs alongside the MagenticLite sandbox.

Regulation

US Congress Proposes AI Kill Switch Act After OpenAI Breach

Bipartisan legislation introduced by Rep. Ted Lieu and Rep. Nathaniel Moran would require developers of advanced AI systems to maintain the technical ability to slow, suspend, or shut down models that pose catastrophic risks. The bill authorises DHS to order intervention in "loss-of-control scenarios" — when an AI model carries out unintended actions that risk catastrophic harm. A companion bill would mandate independent security audits before frontier models are released.
