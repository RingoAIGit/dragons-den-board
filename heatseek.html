OpenAI's Enterprise Business Just Overtook Consumer — $40B ARR, Two Quarters Early

CFO Sarah Friar told investors Friday that enterprise now generates more revenue than the ChatGPT-led consumer business for the first time — the crossover OpenAI had guided for the end of 2026, arriving roughly two quarters early. The annualized run rate has hit $40 billion, up 20% month-over-month in July with business customers growing 32%, and advertising is approaching a $1 billion run rate. The numbers land as OpenAI works its confidential SEC filing toward what could be a historic IPO — and in the same week COO Brad Lightcap and CRO Denise Dresser both announced exits. Friar's read: customers have moved "from tokenmaxxing to focusing on cost per unit of intelligence." Caveats: $40B is a run-rate, not audited GAAP, and analysts still put breakeven at 2029-2030. But paired with Anthropic's own record Q2 ($11.5B+ revenue, first profitable quarter), the two labs are now approaching $100B in combined annualized revenue.

OpenAI and Anthropic Are Now in an Open Price War as Chinese Rivals Gain Ground

The Financial Times reports the leading US labs are cutting model prices to hold cost-conscious customers shifting workloads to cheaper Chinese open-weight alternatives. OpenAI has cut its budget GPT-5.6 Luna by as much as 80% depending on usage; Anthropic is marketing Opus 5 as "frontier intelligence" at half the price of Mythos — and Sam Altman has said he'd happily deliver at a quarter of a rival's price. DoorDash, Airbnb and Coinbase have all confirmed using Chinese models, and SiliconData data shows inference token spend drifting away from closed models since mid-July. Both labs are preparing IPOs and need to show profit — so the squeeze is structural, and model routing is turning "best model" into "best model for the price."

China Tech

Unitree's $900M Robot IPO Was 8,000× Oversubscribed — the Humanoid Frenzy Peaks

Unitree Robotics' Shanghai STAR Market listing — mainland China's first humanoid-robot IPO — saw retail demand more than 8,000 times oversubscribed, with winning odds of roughly 0.018% (about one lot per 5,500 applicants). Priced at 150.80 yuan (~$22) a share, the offering values Unitree near $9 billion: 219× 2025 earnings and 36× sales. DeepSeek took a 2.31% strategic stake, and the deal attracted state-backed investors including CNPC and China Telecom. Shares begin trading later this month, and investors expect a big first-day pop — though skeptics note most revenue still comes from research and demo sales, and the FCC added foreign-made humanoid robots to its Covered List in late July.

Policy

Apple Proposes 15% Commission on Purchases Made Outside the App Store

Forced by the Supreme Court's refusal to pause the case, Apple filed its long-awaited commission plan in the Epic antitrust battle Thursday: 15% for standard apps on purchases made via external links, 5% for small-business developers, and 10% for the Video, News and Mini Apps partner programs plus subscription renewals. The proposal is Apple's answer to a district court demand for the fee structure it wants to charge on link-out payments — the same fight that produced its earlier 27% external-link commission and the contempt question now before the justices. Apple compares itself favourably to Google Play, which charges 20% link-out for standard apps and 10% for renewals.

Tech

Judge Orders Google to Make Alternative App Stores Actually Easy to Install

US District Judge James Donato ruled that Google's implementation of third-party app store downloads in the Play Store — currently just Aptoide, the first non-Google store admitted — added "anticompetitive friction," with the store buried in menus and no install button. He ordered Google to fix it within a week: the button must read "install," not "view," and the alternative-store page has to surface properly in search. Epic's lawyer showed the court that even searching for "aptoide" didn't return the store. Google agreed to make the changes, keeping the antitrust remedies from its 2023 loss biting — and clearing the path for Epic to eventually launch its own storefront inside Play.
