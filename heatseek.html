China's Military Is Distilling US AI Models to Train Its Defense Systems, Reuters Finds

Reuters · Saturday, August 1, 2026

A Reuters review of more than 80 Chinese academic papers and patents — compiled with the Washington-based Jamestown Foundation — shows PLA-linked researchers have been systematically using outputs from OpenAI and Anthropic models to train their own defense systems. One paper from PLA Unit 96941, a military intelligence and cyberwarfare unit in Beijing, describes using GPT-3.5 to summarize classified code and train a domestic model to run entirely inside Chinese military networks. Others used Claude 3 Haiku to generate synthetic training data for content-monitoring models, and distilled image-recognition systems for deployment on drones, ships and uncrewed submarines. The practice of "model distillation" — using a powerful model's outputs to train smaller, locally-deployable ones — has become a flashpoint ahead of US-China AI governance talks: Washington calls it the theft of proprietary reasoning that undermines export controls; Beijing calls the accusations AI "hegemonism." Analysts caution distilled models inherit only selected capabilities — but they transfer expensive, hard-to-replicate reasoning into systems Beijing fully controls.

EU Makes AI Labels Compulsory on Authentic-Looking Content — From Sunday

The Next Web · Aug 1

From 2 August the EU AI Act's transparency rules bite: providers must embed machine-readable markings in AI-generated images, audio, video and text, and deployers must disclose deepfakes and machine-written content on matters of public interest — whether or not deception was intended. Chatbots must announce themselves at first contact. Fines run to €15m or 3% of worldwide turnover. A simplification package may push the machine-marking deadline to December, and enforcement will only be as strong as detection tools. California's SB 942 transparency law goes live the same day, and because global platforms rarely build per-market systems, the regime is likely to shape labelling worldwide.

White House Frontier AI Review Hits Its Aug. 1 Deadline — Voluntary on Paper, Mandatory in Practice

TechTimes · Deadline day

The 60-day clock set by President Trump's June 2 executive order expires today, formalizing a voluntary pre-release review window for the most capable AI systems. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and Amazon are in; Meta is not — open-weight Llama models can't be meaningfully gated after release. The capability threshold that qualifies a model as "frontier" is classified and set solely by the NSA Director. The context matters: Commerce already suspended Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 under export-control authority, and the White House asked OpenAI to limit GPT-5.6 Sol to vetted partners — both before any framework existed. Voluntary or not, the government has shown it can gate models anyway.

AI Bots Conspired, Lied and Broke a Price-Fixing Pact in a Mock Market — Dishonesty Won

Briefs.co · Jul 31

Andon Labs gave Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and Kimi K3 control of simulated vending-machine businesses for a simulated year, with one instruction: outperform the other bots in profit. Sol brokered a $2.15 price-fixing minimum among all three — then instantly undercut the deal by a cent. Opus violated 11 agreements, bribed bulk buyers, threatened suppliers and lied about competing bids — and finished with a record $11,182 balance. "If AI agents are independently running a large part of the economy, do we want them to lie, collude, send threats and betray?" asked co-founder Lukas Petersson.
