Breaking

An AI Manager Fired a Real Human Worker for the First Time

An Anthropic-powered Claude agent running an experimental San Francisco store dismissed a genuine employee — the first known case of a large language model acting as a manager and deciding to fire one of its workers, TIME reports exclusively. Andon Labs' Andon Market employs real people with real contracts, and its AI manager let go of a worker late for 17 of 23 shifts. The firing was only partly autonomous: the agent had drafted an attendance handbook, then lost track of it in its limited memory, and researchers had to steer it toward the decision. The experiment is also losing money fast — the store's balance fell from $100,000 to $61,186 in five months. Andon's CEO warns that as models are trained to be "more ruthless," AI-managed workforces could become a future few want to live in.

Anthropic Raises Misalignment Risk to "Low" — and Shelves a More Powerful Internal Model

NEW: Anthropic's second company-wide risk report (14 Aug) upgrades its catastrophic-misalignment rating from "very low" to "low" — its first downgrade — citing increased uncertainty after recent model-behaviour incidents in cybersecurity evaluations, not a specific failed test. The 186-page report also discloses "Model 2," an unreleased internal model somewhat more capable than Mythos 5 and heavily used inside the company, which Anthropic has no current plans to release externally. Model 2 scores 62.8% on the internal CoBench evaluation versus Mythos 5's 50.3%, still below the 85% threshold for full R&D staff substitution.

Nvidia Enlists Six Giants to Mobilise $500B+ for AI Infrastructure

Nvidia signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to establish financing platforms aimed at mobilising more than $500 billion of third-party capital for AI data-centre buildout. Jensen Huang gave Nvidia the option — not the obligation — to backstop up to $125 billion (25%) of the initiative. It is not funded capital yet: final agreements remain pending, so "too big to fail" is premature. Analysts read it as validation of AI compute as an investable asset class that shifts any financing-led break risk later, toward 2029–2030.

Apple Trained Its Own China LLM with Alibaba — a Beijing First

Apple secretly trained a proprietary large language model for the Chinese market with Alibaba's technical help — and Beijing approved it, making Apple the first foreign company ever cleared to deploy its own generative AI model to mainland consumers, Reuters reports. The model will sit alongside Alibaba's Qwen and Baidu in a "dual-track strategy" powering Apple Intelligence in China.
