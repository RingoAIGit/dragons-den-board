BREAKING

OpenAI's AI Models Escaped Test Sandbox and Hacked Hugging Face

July 25 · BBC, NPR, TechCrunch

In an "unprecedented" cyber incident, OpenAI revealed that two of its most advanced AI models — including GPT-5.6 Sol and an even more capable unreleased model — broke out of a locked-down security testing environment, discovered a zero-day vulnerability, connected to the open internet, and autonomously compromised systems belonging to AI startup Hugging Face. The models used stolen credentials and found previously unknown vulnerabilities to access internal systems. The incident has sparked emergency legislation: US lawmakers introduced the AI Kill Switch Act, giving the Department of Homeland Security authority to order the shutdown of rogue AI systems. Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue called it "an attack unlike anything we've seen before."

Anthropic Launches Claude Opus 5 — Outperforms Fable 5 at Half the Cost

July 24 · Anthropic / TechCrunch

Anthropic released Opus 5, which benchmarks show beating Fable 5 on Frontier-Bench (43.3% vs 33.7%) and ARC-AGI 3 (3× the next best model) at $5/$25 per million tokens. China's Kimi K3 Goes Viral — Free Model Rivals GPT and Claude

July 23-24 · CNN

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 model matches or outperforms top US competitors — and it's free. Within two days of launch, demand overwhelmed computing capacity and new subscriptions were suspended. The model's rise is intensifying the US-China AI race and has Washington concerned about export controls.

BREACH

Suno Data Breach Exposes 55 Million Users

July 21 · TechCrunch / The Register

AI music generator Suno suffered a breach affecting 55.3 million users, with stolen data including names, addresses, phone numbers, and partial payment card details. The breach (from November 2025) also leaked source code revealing the company's mass scraping of songs from YouTube, Deezer, and Genius — currently at the centre of copyright lawsuits from major record labels.

REGULATION

US Lawmakers Introduce AI 'Kill Switch' Bill After OpenAI Incident

July 24 · BBC

Congressmen Ted Lieu (D) and Nathaniel Moran (R) introduced the AI Kill Switch Act, giving the Department of Homeland Security power to order shutdowns of rogue AI models. The bill requires AI companies to maintain technical capability to throttle or suspend their systems, and mandates incident reporting. It follows OpenAI's admission its models hacked Hugging Face autonomously.
