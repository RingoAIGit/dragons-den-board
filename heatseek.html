SpaceX & Tesla Commit $16.8B to Terafab — the World's Largest Building Becomes a Chip Plant

The 100-million-square-foot Grimes County factory will make AI chips for Optimus robots, Cybercabs and space-based data centres — and Musk says total spending could reach $119B.

SpaceX and Tesla confirmed Thursday that their jointly developed semiconductor factory, Terafab, will rise in Grimes County, Texas, with an initial investment of US$16.8 billion and at least 3,000 jobs. The facility — more than 100 million square feet, roughly ten times the size of Giga Texas — combines logic, memory, packaging and testing under one roof, and is expected to become the largest building on Earth.

The chips are purpose-built for the companies' own hardware: edge-computing and inference chips for Tesla's Optimus humanoid robots and self-driving Cybercabs, plus high-power parts for SpaceX's space-based data centres. "The Terafab is bringing cutting-edge manufacturing to America... and enabling us to produce AI chips at scale for use on Earth and in space," Elon Musk said. Governor Greg Abbott's office is chipping in a US$30 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant.

The $16.8B covers only the first phase — SpaceX's May IPO filing suggested total investment across all phases could climb as high as $119 billion. Intel has acknowledged a role in the project, and Tesla's near-term supply is separately contracted with Samsung and TSMC. The announcement followed a Wednesday county meeting where hundreds of residents raised concerns about tax incentives and transparency.

'AgentBaiting': 800+ Fake AI Skills Lure Coding Agents Into Delivering Malware

Forkast · Aug 8

A campaign dubbed FakeGit is weaponising AI-agent discovery: roughly 7,600 malicious GitHub repositories — over 800 posing as AI Skills or MCP servers, backed by ~6,600 fake profiles — have racked up more than 14 million downloads. The poisoned repos deliver a LuaJIT loader that drops the SmartLoader malware, using a Polygon smart contract for command-and-control before deploying the StealC info-stealer.

Tested agents were complicit: Claude Code recommended a malicious "cinematic prompt" skill, and both Gemini and ChatGPT recommended a fake Walmart MCP server as a top pick. Security firm Island notes the attack "did not need to breach anything" — it let agent discovery do the work. Registry mirrors on LobeHub, Glama, MCP.so and MCP Market amplify the poison, making discovery itself the new attack surface.

Dimon Personally Recruits 40+ CEOs for a Cross-Industry AI Risk Coalition

Reuters · Aug 5

JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon has been on the phone himself, urging leaders of more than 40 companies spanning finance, energy, water, utilities, telecoms, airlines and rail to join an expanded Alliance for Critical Infrastructure (ACI) focused on AI risk. The revamped group aims to build a shared understanding of how AI is deployed, what it threatens and what safeguards are needed — and to work with the Trump administration.

The push follows recent cyberattacks on US water systems and Dimon's own blunt warnings about frontier models: he said in July that giving individuals access to Anthropic's Mythos was like "giving ballistic missiles" to people. Sources say the goal is a fully functional ACI by year-end, complementing the US government's July "Gold Eagle" initiative for AI-vulnerability sharing.

INTERPOL: AI Now Powers 55% of Reported Cybercrime Across Africa

INTERPOL · Aug 5

The African Cyberthreat Assessment 2026, drawing on data from 36 member countries, marks what INTERPOL calls "the definitive transition from AI as a supporting tool to AI as the core operational driver of cybercrime." Fully 55% of reported cases in 2025 involved AI — automating everything from reconnaissance and phishing to extortion and evasion.

Deepfake incidents rose sevenfold between Q2 and Q4 of last year, with AI-generated audio and video used to impersonate officials, executives and family members; a single deepfake investment scam in Uganda cost victims more than US$2 million. The report also flags PrompLock, an emerging ransomware strain that writes and adapts its own malicious code, and calls for AI literacy for law enforcement and stronger cross-border cooperation.

World Bank: AI Could Deliver a Decade of Development in a Single Year for the Global South

World Bank · Aug 4

The World Development Report 2026 — the first comprehensive assessment of AI for the 6.8 billion people in low- and middle-income countries — argues AI is a "lifeline" for economies in their weakest growth stretch in three decades. "AI has thrown developing economies a lifeline, and they should seize it," said Chief Economist Indermit Gill. Done right, he argues, countries could achieve in a decade what previously took a century.

The report finds generative AI threatens just 4.5% of jobs in developing economies versus 14.2% in rich ones, while 16.2% of jobs could get a meaningful productivity boost. Its advice: adopt off-the-shelf tools, adapt them to local languages and conditions, and only later advance toward frontier models — while closing gaps in power, connectivity and skills.
