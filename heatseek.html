OpenAI Says Its Next Model May Cross Its Own "Critical" Cyber Line — a First

OpenAI · Axios · Aug 7

OpenAI said Friday it can no longer rule out that Astra, its upcoming flagship, reaches the Critical cybersecurity threshold in its Preparedness Framework — the first time the company has attached that label's possibility to a specific model. Days of internal evaluations showed "significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity"; every prior model, including GPT-5.6 Sol, sat at High.

Under the framework, Critical means a model can develop functional zero-day exploits against hardened real-world systems without human intervention — a "qualitatively new threat vector with no ready precedent." OpenAI has begun implementing stricter controls (isolated test environments, encrypted weight protections, chain-of-thought monitoring), paused internal Astra work that doesn't yet meet the new bar, and will bring government agencies and outside safety organisations into testing. No release date was named. Astra was not involved in the July Hugging Face breach, the company stressed.

CNBC · Time · Axios · Aug 5–6

AI Writes Viruses Evolution Never Made — 16 Synthetic Phages Come Alive in the Lab

Science · Stanford & Arc Institute · Aug 7

Stanford and the Arc Institute report in Science that Evo 2, a genome language model trained on DNA the way LLMs are trained on text, designed complete viral genomes from scratch — and 16 of them, once synthesised and introduced to E. coli, produced fully functional bacteriophages that infected and killed their host. It's the first peer-reviewed demonstration of generative AI designing entire functional viral genomes. Johns Hopkins biosecurity specialists warn existing synthetic-DNA screening cannot detect novel AI-written sequences; pending US legislation still leans on similarity-based screening critics say is blind to exactly these genomes.

Meta Enters the Coding Wars: Muse Code Undercuts Claude Code and Codex

Quartz · WSJ · Neowin · Aug 5

Meta released Muse Code in beta Wednesday — a terminal coding agent powered by the new Muse Spark 1.2, trained alongside the harness. It plans changes, edits files across large repos, runs commands and tests, and spins up parallel subagents in isolated worktrees so your working copy stays untouched. The pricing is the headline: the standard pay-as-you-go tier undercuts rivals, and a "contributor" tier — where developers consent to Meta training on their data — is 10–21× cheaper, per AI chief Alexandr Wang. Meta's own charts concede Claude Opus 5 leads on capability; the play is price and architecture. One-command install on macOS and Linux.

37,000 AI Agents Run a Virtual Biotech — Merck Independently Confirmed Its Drug Design

VentureBeat · VB Transform · Aug 7

At VB Transform, Stanford's James Zou detailed the Virtual Biotech: tens of thousands of specialised AI agents — target discovery, molecule design, clinical trials — overseen by a Chief Scientific Officer agent, orchestrating via Paperclip, which maps databases into an AI-native file system. Spinning up 37,000 "clinical trial agents" to synthesise fragmented trial data, the system autonomously designed an antibody-drug conjugate targeting CD276 for lung cancer, using only data published before January 2025. Months later, Merck independently developed and validated the same design — which then earned FDA breakthrough designation. Zou's argument: the frontier isn't one smarter agent, it's tens of thousands collaborating.
