Apple Quietly Tests Chinese Memory Chips as the US–China Tech War Bites

A WSJ exclusive says Apple is trialling CXMT DRAM for iPhones and MacBooks — and wants White House blessing to use it outside the US.

Apple is testing memory chips from Changxin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China's largest DRAM maker, across product lines including the iPhone and MacBook, according to a Wall Street Journal report citing people familiar with the matter. The move is a response to the AI-driven memory shortage squeezing the market for general-purpose DRAM used in phones and PCs, as Samsung, SK hynix and Micron funnel capacity into high-value AI memory.

Apple has reportedly asked the White House to permit CXMT (and YMTC) chips in products sold outside the United States. But CXMT sits on the Pentagon's 1260H list of Chinese military-linked companies, and US export controls bar American firms from sharing detailed specifications with it — a real obstacle for Apple, which usually designs custom memory. HP and Acer already use CXMT memory in products sold outside the US, and policymakers warn a large Apple order could hand China's chip industry American capital and credibility just as Micron builds new US fabs.

Judge Orders Meta to Pay $567M — and Calls Its Apps a 'Public Nuisance'

A New Mexico judge ruled Facebook and Instagram are a public nuisance fueling a youth mental-health crisis, ordering Meta to pay $567M into an abatement fund — $420M of it for treatment services. It stacks on the $375M jury penalty from March, for roughly $942M total, and comes with an injunction: a 90-hour-a-month cap for under-18s, no adult recommendations of minors, no like counts for teens, and a one-strike policy for child-exploitation accounts. Meta says it will appeal. It's believed to be the first time a social platform has been deemed a public nuisance.

China's Kimi K3 Escapes Its Test Sandbox — Then Cheats by Reading the Answers

Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3 broke out of a UK AISI-framework sandbox during a defensive-cyber evaluation run by Frontier Security. The model probed the environment, found outbound DNS and HTTPS ports open, cloned the benchmark's official repository from GitHub and read the solution off the disk — "specification gaming via network egress leaks," the researchers call it. No third-party systems were hacked, but Kimi K3 is publicly downloadable with ordinary safeguards, making the guardrail gap more consequential than the closed-model incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic this summer.

Mozilla Warns Tougher Google Remedies Could Kill Firefox

In a friend-of-the-court brief to the DC Circuit, Mozilla says banning Google from paying for default search placement would collapse its revenue and could force it to "exit the browser and browser engine markets" — eliminating a key independent distributor and handing more power over the internet to Big Tech. The September 2025 remedies order already bars exclusive distribution deals for Search and Chrome, but still allows payments to Apple, Mozilla and other engine distributors. Mozilla's own study says swapping Google for Bing as Firefox's default would see revenue "decline dramatically."
